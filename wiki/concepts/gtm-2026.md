# GTM 2026 (World-Class GTM in the AI Era)

## Definition
The set of go-to-market frames articulated by 2026's top operators (most canonically by [[jeanne-dewitt-grosser]] on [[lenny-rachitsky]]'s podcast) describing how sales, marketing, customer success, and revenue ops should be structured in the AI era. Three core moves distinguish 2026 GTM from 2024 GTM:

1. **GTM gets more strategically important, not less** — automation does not eliminate GTM; it raises the table-stakes for it
2. **The GTM engineer is the new shape** — a hybrid sales/marketing-ops + software engineer role, building the internal tooling that the AI-augmented GTM org runs on
3. **Build vs buy has shifted** — the AI era cheapens internal builds enough that the calculus most teams default to ("buy from a vendor") deserves re-examination

## Origin
Crystallized in [[lenny-rachitsky]] × [[jeanne-dewitt-grosser]] *What world-class GTM looks like in 2026* ([[youtube-digest-2026-05-03-r3]] #5, 1:26:02, 80.7K views, 2025-11-30). Jeanne is uniquely credible: built Stripe's early sales org from scratch, prior tenure at Google, currently COO at Vercel — three of the highest-credibility developer-tools GTM organizations.

## Key claims (from Jeanne DeWitt Grosser interview)

### 1. GTM is MORE strategically important in the AI era
- Automation/agent narratives create the illusion that GTM is being eliminated
- The opposite is true: when product is increasingly commoditized via AI, GTM becomes the durable differentiator
- Implication for AI-native startups: under-investing in GTM because "the product sells itself" is a category error

### 2. The rise of the GTM engineer
- A new role profile fusing sales/marketing operations with software engineering
- Technical enough to ship internal tooling (CRM customizations, dashboards, agentic outreach systems, lead-scoring models)
- GTM-fluent enough to design the workflow that tooling supports
- This is the role profile [[ai-consulting]] practices end up serving — clients hire 3rd-party operators because they don't have a GTM engineer in-house yet
- See [[claude-code]] for the substrate this role operates on

### 3. Segmentation is foundational
- Jeanne offers a "primer on segmentation" — most teams skip it, then later wonder why their messaging/pricing/ICP/comp plan doesn't cohere
- Specific frameworks not transcribed yet (transcript at lennysnewsletter.com worth ingesting separately)

### 4. Sales orgs need to be respected by engineering and product
- Designable goal, not an accident of culture
- Specific habits earn the respect — implies a checklist exists in the interview
- Critical for developer-tools companies (Vercel, Stripe) where engineering is the customer

### 5. Build vs buy has shifted in the AI era
- What used to default to "buy from a vendor" is increasingly cheap to build
- The new question: when DOES it still make sense to buy?
- Heuristics not fully transcribed; worth ingesting full transcript for the working frame

### 6. Pain > upside in buying psychology
- **Most customers buy to avoid pain rather than to gain upside**
- Cascades into pricing, positioning, and messaging:
  - Pricing should target the cost of the unsolved pain, not the value of the upside
  - Positioning leads with what's broken, not what could be amazing
  - Messaging emphasizes loss aversion / risk avoidance over aspirational gain
- Counter-intuitive for AI tools that often pitch "10x productivity" upside framing

## Contrasts with

- **2010s SaaS GTM playbook** — high-velocity sales, content-marketing-driven inbound, tightly-orchestrated funnel — depends on observable intent that agents are now hiding
- **"PMF is enough" / product-led-growth purism** — Jeanne's frame says PLG without GTM engineering plateaus; you still need the org and the role to scale past it
- **Vendor-bundle "GTM tech stack"** (Salesforce + HubSpot + Outreach + Clay + ZoomInfo + ...) — build-vs-buy shift means the stack-bundle assumption is suspect

## Open questions / disagreements

- **Pain vs upside in AI-native segments** — some AI tools sell aspirational upside successfully (e.g. Cursor, Lovable, Claude Code itself). Is "pain > upside" universally true or segment-dependent?
- **GTM engineer org placement** — does the role report into Sales, Marketing, RevOps, or its own function? Jeanne's view not yet transcribed
- **Build threshold for AI-era tools** — how cheap does a build need to be for "buy" to lose? Order-of-magnitude not specified
- **AI-buyer GTM** ([[agentic-commerce]]) — Jeanne's frame is human-buyer-centric; agent-buyer GTM is the next layer (per [[nate-b-jones]] #28)
