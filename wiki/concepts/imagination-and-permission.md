# Imagination & Permission

## Definition

[[nate-b-jones]]' framework from *You Can't Compete on Cheap Models Anymore* (43.9K views, 2026-07-05, 15:39) in [[sources/youtube-digest-apify-2026-07-06]] — the **37th** in his tracked cadence (after [[model-routing]] #35 and [[reusable-agent-skeleton]] #36). It is the **demand-side companion** to his supply-side [[model-routing]] picker: model-routing answered *"which model for which job"*; this framework answers *"cheap routing is now solved — so what actually separates you?"*

The framing problem:

> *"Everyone in AI is saying the same thing right now: route your work to cheaper models. It's the right call — and it's about to be table stakes, which means the real advantage is moving to a layer almost nobody is budgeting for."*

The reframe:

> *"The common story is that cheap models turn good work into a commodity — but the real question is who can imagine the work that isn't on anyone's list yet."*

The punchline / diagnostic:

> *"Keep routing execution to cheap models — but if your task list hasn't changed in a year, your constraint on AI returns is imagination and permission, not price."*

## The core move: value moved from supply to demand

The argument runs in three steps:

1. **Execution is commoditizing.** On routine work a **$1 model now ties a $9 model** (the *"$40 test,"* chapter 00:49). When the cheap model matches the expensive one on the routine majority, **price stops being a moat.**
2. **So routing is table stakes, not an edge.** Everyone is already saying "route to cheaper models" ([[model-routing]]); once everyone does it, it can't be where returns come from.
3. **The advantage snaps back to the demand side.** The binding constraint becomes **what work you can imagine and are permitted to build** — *"the work that isn't on anyone's list yet."* That layer is *"almost nobody is budgeting for."*

## Cheap engine, frontier steering

The framework's prescriptive core (chapter 04:06) is **not** "use the cheapest model" — it's *"run execution on a cheap engine and steer with a frontier model."* This is the same shape as [[harness-over-model]] (the harness beats the raw score) and the [[claude-fable-5|goal-harness move]] (use the frontier model to *build the harness that steers a cheaper coding model*), but stated as a **competitive** argument rather than a quality one: cheap-engine/frontier-steering is what everyone converges on, so the *steering* — the imagination behind what you point the engine at — is the remaining differentiator.

## The BlackBerry / Apple multiplier

The historical analogy (chapter 05:38): BlackBerry and Apple had roughly **equal execution capability**, but Apple **imagined a different task list** (a general-purpose app platform vs a better email device). With execution commoditized, *imagination + permission to build the new thing* is the multiplier that separates equal executors — a portable client-deck framing for the whole thesis.

## Where "permission" comes in

The newly explicit second half is **permission** — the *organizational and political* freedom to change a task list, not merely the imagination to see the new work. You can imagine the unlisted job and still be blocked from building it (no mandate, no budget line, no authority). Jones pairs the two because the constraint is rarely capability now: it's *(a)* seeing the work no one has listed and *(b)* being allowed to do it. This connects to his standing organizational-readiness thread — [[public-ai-work]] (the apprenticeship gap), [[agent-ownership]] (someone must own the agent), and [[software-abundance-pm]] (judgment relocates up the stack).

## Where it sits in the vault

- **The demand-side counterweight to [[model-routing]]** (both [[nate-b-jones]], three days apart). Read as one argument: *route the cheap 80% (supply-side picker), then compete on imagined work the routing can't touch (demand-side constraint).*
- **Promotes [[claude-fable-5|task imagination]] to a standalone framework** — first an aside in *The Doing Got Cheap* ("the doing is cheap, the deciding is not"), now the load-bearing thesis of a dedicated video, with **permission** added.
- **A [[harness-over-model]] restatement in competitive terms** — *cheap engine, frontier steering* is harness-beats-score, reframed from "why the score doesn't pick your daily driver" to "why price won't be your moat."
- **A commoditization datapoint for [[model-routing]]** — *"a $1 model ties a $9 model on routine work"* is the empirical hinge under "route the familiar 80% to a cheap workhorse."
- **A [[free-sample-phase]] adjacency** — the same substrate-economics backdrop (cheap models racing to parity) that makes routing table stakes.

## Why it matters for 3Ps

- **A billable diagnostic** — *"has your task list changed in a year?"* is a clean discovery exercise: surface the jobs a client *isn't* doing because they were never on the list, not the ones they're doing slightly cheaper.
- **Reframes the AI-savings pitch** — cost savings from cheap-model routing are real but *converging to zero as a differentiator*; the durable consulting value is helping a client **imagine and get permission for** the new task list. That's a strategy engagement, not a cost-optimization one.
- **The BlackBerry/Apple slide is deck-ready** — equal execution, imagination as the multiplier — for any "why isn't AI moving our numbers?" conversation.
