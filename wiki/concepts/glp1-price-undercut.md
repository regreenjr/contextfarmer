# GLP-1 Price-Undercut Ad (Competitor Price-Comparison)

## What it is

A DTC creative pattern in which a compounded-GLP-1 telehealth challenger **names the incumbents' prices side by side** and anchors its own (lower) price against them, closing on an active-ingredient-equivalence claim. First surfaced in the [[competitor-ads-farm]] on 2026-06-08 (batch 19) via **Rx Pros Wellness Online**.

## The canonical instance (Rx Pros Wellness Online, 2026-06-08)

> Ro charges $400. Hims charges $380. Mochi runs $350. We deliver compounded tirzepatide for $144.97/month. Same active ingredient. Different price tag — and yes, you read that right.

- **Advertiser:** Rx Pros Wellness Online (compounded-tirzepatide telehealth)
- **Ad ID:** `1397556809081453`, started 2026-05-22, no headline
- **Surfaced via:** the bare-"Ro" substring filter ("P-**ro**-s") — classified as *noise* by the farm, yet the highest-creative-intel single ad surfaced to date

## The structure

```
[Incumbent A] charges [$high].
[Incumbent B] charges [$mid].
[Incumbent C] runs [$lower].
We deliver [same product class] for [$lowest]/month.
[Active-ingredient-equivalence claim].
[Disbelief / contrast punch].
```

Four moves:

1. **Price ladder, named** — list 2-3 named incumbents at their real (high) prices, descending, so the reader's anchor is set high before the reveal.
2. **The drop** — your price, stated as a hard monthly number well below the ladder (here $144.97 vs $350-400 — a ~60% undercut).
3. **Equivalence neutralizer** — *"Same active ingredient"* — pre-empts the "you get what you pay for" objection by asserting the product is pharmacologically identical (compounded tirzepatide = the molecule in Zepbound/Mounjaro).
4. **Contrast punch** — *"Different price tag — and yes, you read that right"* — names the cognitive dissonance directly and dares the reader to disbelieve.

## Why it's distinct from the other farm patterns

- **vs the Hims [[dtc-telehealth-ad-template]]** (three-bullet skeleton): Hims **never names a competitor's price** — its equivalence claims are against *branded reference drugs* ("Same active ingredients as Viagra® and Cialis®"), not against rival telehealth platforms' prices. The three-bullet template sells legitimacy (does it work / can I trust the provider / will it ship); the price-undercut sells **relative value** against named rivals. Opposite persuasion axis.
- **vs the long-form confession contrast pattern** (Lauren Brooks / Cholesterol narratives): those are emotional, story-led, failure-anchored. The price-undercut is **terse, numeric, comparison-led** — closer to a B2B feature-vs-price table rendered as ad copy.
- **vs catalog placeholders** (Ro / OpenAI / Anthropic `{{product.brand}}`): no narrative at all. The price-undercut is maximally explicit narrative.

## Why it matters for [[medvi-positioning]]

This is a **near-exact template for a Medvi price-anchored compete creative.** Medvi is the same shape as Rx Pros — compounded GLP-1, undercutting branded/incumbent pricing. The four-move structure transfers directly:

- Name the incumbents Medvi undercuts (Hims, Ro, Henry Meds, Mochi) at their real prices
- Anchor Medvi's monthly price below the ladder
- Neutralize quality skepticism with the active-ingredient-equivalence claim (compounded semaglutide/tirzepatide = the branded molecule)
- Punch the contrast

**Caveat — compliance:** naming competitors and making equivalence claims raises the regulatory/legal bar. The active-ingredient-equivalence claim must pair with the [[compounded-drug-disclaimer]] (FDA does not approve/verify compounded drugs), and competitor price claims must be substantiable and current. Rx Pros ships the claim *without* a visible disclaimer in the captured copy — a risk Medvi should not copy. The structure is the template; the compliance wrapper is mandatory.

## Methodology note — signal/noise is orthogonal to creative-intel value

Rx Pros was classified **noise** by the farm's bare-brand-substring filter (it matched on "Pros," not a tracked page-ID), yet by creative-intel value it outranks every placeholder "signal" ad in 19 batches. The lesson for the [[competitor-ads-farm]]: page-ID allow-listing (the proposed filter fix) would have **dropped** this ad. The right fix is a **separate compounded-GLP-1-challenger watch-list** (Rx Pros, Mochi, + future surfacers) running alongside the tracked-brand allow-list — converting the highest-intel creative from accidental noise into tracked signal.
