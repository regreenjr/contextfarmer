# Mid-Market AI Agency

## Definition

The agency-scale AI consulting thesis named by [[devin-kearns]] (co-founder & CEO of Custom AI Studio) in [[nate-herk]]'s 1:44:03 long-form interview *The Playbook for a $100M AI Agency* (13.6K views, 2026-05-25). Argues that the **mid-market** ($5M-$250M/yr revenue) is the prime opportunity for AI agencies — structurally protected from both [[claude-for-small-business]] commoditization (which eats SMB) and McKinsey-tier consulting (which dominates enterprise).

Source: [[youtube-digest-apify-2026-05-26]] #2. Sits at the **agency-scale top** of the [[ai-consulting]] tier ladder, above [[ai-operating-system-offer]] (zero-to-first-customer rung) and [[chief-ai-officer]] (single-seat scale).

## The three-tier market thesis (chapter 3 — "The Big Bet", 32:14)

| Tier | Revenue size | Why most agencies pick it | Why Devin doesn't recommend it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SMB** (<$5M/yr) | Small business, solopreneur | Easy access, lots of them | Low budget, high churn, high education cost, won't survive 2027 commoditization (per [[claude-for-small-business]]) |
| **Mid-market** ($5M – $250M/yr) | Real operational complexity, small enough to land | **The prime opportunity** | — |
| **Enterprise** (>$1B/yr) | Fortune-500-tier | High contract value | 12-18 month sales cycle, RFP-driven, dominated by McKinsey-tier; agencies can't compete |

## Why mid-market is the prime opportunity

- **Real operational complexity** (worth automating) but not yet **procurement complexity** (multi-quarter sales cycles)
- **Decision-maker is reachable** — CEO/COO/CFO, not 6 layers deep
- **Budget exists** — $50K-$500K engagements below board approval, above SMB pain threshold
- **Won't be killed by [[claude-for-small-business]]** — CFSB targets the SMB long-tail with vertical plugins; mid-market needs *custom* operational deployment that doesn't fit the plugin shape
- **Won't be killed by McKinsey** — enterprises stay with MBB; mid-market is too small for them to focus on
- **Integration complexity > vertical-plugin reach** — the [[agentic-implementation-layer]] five durable primitives (workflow design + data access + authority + evals + audit trails) all require client-specific deployment that vertical plugins can't deliver

## "Most AI work sold today won't survive 2027" (chapter 1 — "The Inflection Point", 5:43)

The framing claim. Three forces compress the commodity AI-consulting market:

| Force | What it does | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontier-model capability jump** | Absorbs commodity prompting / one-shot agent builds | Workflow design + harness |
| **Vertical plugin distribution** | Anthropic / OpenAI ship vertical plugins ([[claude-for-small-business]]) eating long-tail SMB | Custom mid-market deployment |
| **Adoption / education catchup** | 61-point CAIO adoption gap closes ([[chief-ai-officer]]) | Specialized expertise, not generic training |

The 2027 timeline is **more aggressive** than prior vault framings of the AI-creator gold-rush runway (which generally treated 2026-2028 as sustained runway).

## "Position with frameworks, not as a vendor" (chapter 5 — "The Playbook", 1:07:09)

The positioning rationale that operationalizes the agency-scale thesis:

| Positioning | Competes on | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor** | Price + features | Commoditized — race to bottom |
| **Framework** | Interpretation + judgment | Durable — buyer pays for *how to think* |

Same as [[prove-it-economy]]' truth-layer thesis at the agency-positioning scale. Same as [[nate-b-jones]]' framework-per-video flywheel — **21 frameworks in 22 days** produces inbound consulting demand without selling.

The framework-positioning strategy is **portable** to any agency in any vertical — not just AI consulting. Mid-market clients buy frameworks because they have the operational complexity to apply them but not the in-house staff to develop them.

## Strategic significance

1. **First explicit "mid-market is the agency wedge" thesis in this vault** — prior [[ai-consulting]] coverage spanned SMB / pre-revenue / mid-tier offer-language / macro-thesis / MBB but never named mid-market ($5M-$250M) as the strategic sweet spot.
2. **Closes the [[ai-consulting]] tier ladder end-to-end** — Devin/Custom AI Studio at agency-scale ($100M target) + [[ai-operating-system-offer]] at zero-to-first-customer + [[nicole-mccain]] at pre-revenue beginner + [[ramin-imani]] at MBB-aspirant + [[mark-kashef]] at macro thesis + [[mert-yerlikaya]] at mid-tier offer-language. **Eleven voices** now visible end-to-end across the wedge.
3. **The 2027 timeline is the most aggressive runway claim in the vault** — shortens the commoditization window from "2026-2028 sustained" to "12-18 months." Operational implication: mid-market positioning needs to happen *now*.
4. **The "frameworks not vendor" positioning is the explicit rationale** for every framework-per-video content production strategy in the vault — [[nate-b-jones]] (21 frameworks), [[nate-herk]] (7+ operational frameworks), [[simon-scrapes]] (skill-systems + self-improving-skills), [[brad-bonanno]] (execution-layer). The flywheel had been observed; this concept names *why* it works.
5. **Mid-market is structurally the 3Ps target** — the $5M-$250M segment is exactly where 3Ps consulting deliverables fit. Devin's framework names the buyer.
6. **Custom AI Studio is a direct competitive benchmark** for any agency-scale offer 3Ps might run.
