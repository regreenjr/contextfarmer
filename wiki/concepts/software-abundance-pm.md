# Software-Abundance PM (Production Class Ladder)

## Definition

[[nate-b-jones]]'s **25th framework** — a redefinition of the **product-management role** once AI collapses the cost of building software. The core claim: prototyping is table stakes; the durable PM job is **classifying software abundance into market value** — deciding which of the flood of cheap, already-built software a company should actually *rely on*.

Source: [[nate-b-jones]] *Cheap software made your PM job harder, not easier. Here's the new job.* (11.4K views, 2026-05-29, 12:38), surfaced via [[youtube-digest-apify-2026-05-30]]. Full post + prompts gated to `natesnewsletter.substack.com`.

## The framing claim

> *"The common story is that PMs just become prototypers — but the reality is more complicated. Prototyping is table stakes. The real shift is that AI moved the bottleneck, and product management is becoming the discipline that classifies software abundance into market value."*

The signature *"the common story is X, but the reality is Y"* structure, applied to the PM function.

## The artifact arrives before the request

The central inversion. In the old world, a request preceded the artifact: someone *asked* for software, then it got built, and the PM's gate was **"should I build this?"** In the cheap-software world, **the artifact arrives first** — someone (or an agent) has already built the thing. So the PM's question flips to:

> **"Somebody already built something — should the company actually rely on it?"**

The bottleneck moved from *building* to *governing what got built*.

## Microsoft's million-asset reality check (chapter 1:14)

The named exemplar: **Microsoft already has more than 1 million internal Power Platform assets** built inside the company. Software abundance is not hypothetical — it is already the lived reality inside large orgs. This flood is the **prototype commons** (chapter ~3:45–5:20): a shared pool of cheaply-built software that creates both:

- **Hidden demand** — capabilities now exist that nobody formally requested, surfacing latent needs.
- **Hidden risk** — assets the org may come to depend on without anyone vetting reliability, security, or ownership. *"Broad building meets governance risk"* (chapter 5:20).

## Why the non-technical PM is finished (chapter 2:30)

The role's old filter — translating between business and engineering — breaks down (chapter 3:45). A PM who can **neither build nor govern** has no remaining filter to add. Prototyping ability is now table stakes; on top of it, the new scarce skill is **governance + market judgment**. The non-technical PM "is running out of room."

## The production class ladder

A **tiered reliance model** for cheap software (detailed in the gated Substack post). Each asset in the prototype commons sits at some rung of how much the company *relies on* it — from throwaway prototype up to production-critical dependency. The framework's distinctive move:

> **Demotion matters as much as promotion.**

The PM's job isn't only to *promote* assets up the ladder (ship more, rely more). Moving an asset **down** — deprecate it, sandbox it, stop relying on it — is now a first-class PM act. In a world of software abundance, **subtracting** dependencies is as valuable as adding them. (Specifics of the ladder's tiers are transcript/Substack-gated.)

## Market judgment is the new scarce thing (chapter 6:40)

When building is cheap and abundant, the scarce, durable skill is **judgment about which abundance creates market value**. This is the PM-function version of [[nate-b-jones]]'s recurring thesis that the model/tool is commoditizing and the *judgment layer* is where value concentrates (cf. [[long-running-benchmarks]]' "harness is the real story," [[agent-analytics]]' "the correction is your most valuable signal").

## Where it sits among his frameworks

| Framework | Side | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **T/C/L/D** | Worker | Which of *my* work is durable? |
| [[capital-allocation-framework]] | Decision | Automate / build / buy / hire / wait on a workflow? |
| **[[software-abundance-pm]]** (this) | **PM role / consumer-of-abundance** | **Which already-built software should we rely on?** |

The framework is the **consumer-of-abundance mirror** of his [[work-primitive]] (producer-of-action: access / meaning / authority). Where capital-allocation asks *should we build/buy this workflow?*, software-abundance-PM asks *given it's already built, should we depend on it?* — a downstream governance question that only exists once building is cheap.

His **25th framework in 26 days** (T/C/L/D 2026-05-04 → software-abundance-PM 2026-05-29) — and his first explicit **role-redefinition** framework aimed at the **product-management** function.

## Strategic significance for 3Ps

1. **A governance-side consulting deliverable** — a "production class ladder audit" of a client's internal AI/prototype sprawl is a clean engagement, the same shape as the T/C/L/D week-tag and the [[agent-metering]] pre-renewal review.
2. **"Demotion matters as much as promotion"** is a sharp, sellable line — most AI-adoption advice is additive (build more); the *subtraction* discipline is under-served.
3. **The prototype commons names a real enterprise pain** — orgs with [[claude-for-small-business]]-style internal sprawl need someone to govern what to rely on. That governance role *is* a 3Ps wedge.
