# Devin Kearns

## What it is

Co-founder & CEO of **Custom AI Studio**, an AI agency targeting **mid-market** ($5M-$250M/yr) clients. YouTube channel: **@customaistudio**. Tracked in this vault as the first **agency-scale voice** in the AI-consulting wedge — sits above the zero-to-first-customer / pre-revenue / mid-tier / macro-thesis / MBB-survival voices already mapped.

Surfaced via [[nate-herk]]'s long-form interview *The Playbook for a $100M AI Agency* (13.6K views, 2026-05-25, 1:44:03), the longest single video in this vault and Nate Herk's first long-form interview format.

## Why he matters for this wiki

1. **First agency-scale operator voice** — prior [[ai-consulting]] coverage was solo-operator / beginner-coach / macro-thesis. Devin runs Custom AI Studio at agency scale with a $100M revenue target.
2. **Originates the [[mid-market-ai-agency]] thesis** — *"mid-market is the prime opportunity, not SMBs or enterprises"* — fills the gap between [[claude-for-small-business]] commoditization (SMB) and McKinsey-tier enterprise consulting.
3. **Names the 2027 commoditization timeline** — *"most AI work being sold today won't survive 2027"* — more aggressive than prior vault framings of the AI-creator gold-rush runway.
4. **The "11 ways AI experts make money" portfolio** — taxonomy of revenue models including custom builds, retainers, fractional CAIO, training, AI-native SaaS, affiliates, done-for-you, audits, AI-enabled services, content monetization, and roll-up acquisitions.
5. **"Position with frameworks, not as a vendor"** — explicit positioning rationale for the framework-per-video content production strategy that [[nate-b-jones]] and [[nate-herk]] both run.
6. **Closes the [[ai-consulting]] tier ladder end-to-end** — 11 voices now visible across the wedge.

## Key claims (from [[youtube-digest-apify-2026-05-26]] #2)

### The three-tier market thesis (chapter 3, ~32:14 — "The Big Bet")

| Tier | Revenue size | Why most agencies pick it | Why Devin doesn't recommend it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SMB** (<$5M/yr) | Small business, solopreneur | Easy to access, lots of them | Low budget, high churn, high education cost, won't survive 2027 commoditization |
| **Mid-market** ($5M – $250M/yr) | Real operational complexity but small enough to land | **The prime opportunity** | — |
| **Enterprise** (>$1B/yr) | Fortune-500-tier | High contract value | 12-18 month sales cycle, dominated by McKinsey-tier; agencies can't compete |

### Why mid-market is the prime opportunity

- **Real operational complexity** worth automating
- **Decision-maker is reachable** — CEO/COO/CFO, not 6 layers deep
- **Budget exists** — $50K-$500K engagements below board approval, above SMB pain threshold
- **Won't be killed by [[claude-for-small-business]]** — CFSB targets SMB long-tail; mid-market needs custom deployment
- **Won't be killed by McKinsey** — enterprises stay with MBB; mid-market too small for them to focus on

### "Most AI work sold today won't survive 2027" (chapter 1: The Inflection Point, 5:43)

- Frontier models absorb commodity prompting / one-shot agent builds
- Anthropic / OpenAI ship vertical plugins ([[claude-for-small-business]]) eating the long tail
- Mid-market custom deployment **survives** because integration / context / workflow / authority cannot be commoditized at vertical-plugin scale
- The [[agentic-implementation-layer]] five durable primitives (workflow design + data access + authority + evals + audit trails) is what survives

### "Position with frameworks, not as a vendor" (chapter 5: The Playbook, 1:07:09)

- Vendors compete on price + features (commoditized)
- Frameworks compete on interpretation + judgment (durable)
- Same as [[prove-it-economy]]' truth-layer thesis at the agency-positioning scale
- Same as [[nate-b-jones]] framework-per-video flywheel — 21 frameworks in 22 days produces inbound consulting demand

### The 11 ways AI experts are making money (chapter map gated; inferred portfolio)

1. Custom AI builds (project-based — Custom AI Studio core)
2. AI-Operating-System retainers (the [[ai-operating-system-offer]] rung 4 equivalent at agency scale)
3. Fractional CAIO seats ([[chief-ai-officer]] external-operator path)
4. AI training / education (cohorts, courses, workshops)
5. AI-native SaaS (productized agent products)
6. Affiliate / partnership revenue (Skool, Hostinger, Glaido, KIE, etc.)
7. Done-for-you implementation (workflow + harness delivery)
8. Audit / advisory (one-day deliverables)
9. AI-enabled service businesses (use AI to deliver non-AI service cheaper — e.g., [[eric-tech]]' bookzero.ai)
10. Content / community monetization (YouTube + Skool + newsletter flywheel — [[nate-herk]] / [[nate-b-jones]] / [[simon-scrapes]])
11. Acquisition / roll-up (buy small AI agencies and consolidate)

### Five things he wishes he knew sooner (gated; inferred)

Likely pricing structure (chapter 2 Economics, 17:34), market selection (chapter 3 Big Bet), starting-from-zero playbook (chapter 4, 49:46), framework-positioning (chapter 5), and capacity / team building.
