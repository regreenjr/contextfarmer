## 1. Nate B Jones — once cheap routing is table stakes, the constraint is imagination, not price ([[nate-b-jones]], 43.9K views, 15:39)

*You Can't Compete on Cheap Models Anymore.* → **New concept: [[imagination-and-permission]].** His next framework (the **37th** in the vault's cadence, after [[model-routing]] #35 and [[reusable-agent-skeleton]] #36) and the **demand-side companion** to his own [[model-routing]] picker. Where model-routing answered *"which model for which job"* on the supply side, this video answers *"so cheap routing is solved — now what actually separates you?"*

The framing problem (from the description):

> *"Everyone in AI is saying the same thing right now: route your work to cheaper models. It's the right call — and it's about to be table stakes, which means the real advantage is moving to a layer almost nobody is budgeting for."*

The reframe:

> *"The common story is that cheap models turn good work into a commodity — but the real question is who can imagine the work that isn't on anyone's list yet."*

### The load-bearing claims

- **Execution is commoditizing** — *"a $1 model now ties a $9 model on routine work,"* so *"cheap models turn good work into a commodity."* Routing to cheap models is *"the right call and about to be table stakes."*
- **Value moved to the demand side** — the real question is *"who can imagine the work that isn't on anyone's list yet."* This is his [[claude-fable-5|task-imagination]] thesis promoted from a model-review aside to a **standalone competitive framework**.
- **Cheap engine, frontier steering** — the durable operating shape is not "use the cheapest model" but "run execution cheap and **steer with a frontier model**" — the same [[harness-over-model|harness-beats-score]] / goal-harness move, now stated as a *competitive* rather than a *quality* argument.
- **The BlackBerry/Apple multiplier** — with *"equal execution,"* what separated them was **imagination + permission to build a different task list**, not who executed better.
- **The punchline** — *"keep routing execution to cheap models, but if your task list hasn't changed in a year, your constraint on AI returns is imagination and permission, not price."*

Strategic read: this closes a loop Jones has been circling for weeks. [[claude-fable-5]] said *"the doing got cheap; the deciding did not"* and named **task imagination** as the new core skill; [[model-routing]] shipped the supply-side picker; **this video fuses them** — because everyone will route to cheap models, routing is no longer where returns come from, so the binding constraint snaps back to the demand side: **what work you can imagine and are permitted to build.** *"Permission"* is the newly explicit second half — the organizational/political freedom to change a task list, not just the imagination to see the new work. → Updates: new [[imagination-and-permission]]; [[model-routing]] (demand-side companion), [[harness-over-model]] (cheap-engine-frontier-steering), [[claude-fable-5]] (task-imagination promoted to a framework), [[nate-b-jones]].
