## "Noise" inventory — 2 of 2 ads (100%), but one is the most valuable creative in the farm

This is the farm's first noise-only batch since batch 6 — yet it carries the **highest-intel single ad** surfaced to date. The two pages:

### Rx Pros Wellness Online — NEW page; the competitor price-undercut find

- **Substring root:** "P-**ro**-s" in "Pros" (bare-"Ro" filter match)
- **ID:** `1397556809081453`, started **2026-05-22**, format unknown, no headline
- **Primary text:** *"Ro charges $400. Hims charges $380. Mochi runs $350. We deliver compounded tirzepatide for $144.97/month. Same active ingredient. Different price tag — and yes, you read that right."*

**Why this is the most Medvi-relevant ad the farm has surfaced:** Rx Pros is a **compounded-tirzepatide telehealth challenger** — exactly Medvi's shape — running a **direct competitor price-comparison undercut** that names three tracked/adjacent incumbents (Ro $400, Hims $380, Mochi $350) and anchors its own $144.97/month against them with an **ingredient-equivalence claim** (*"Same active ingredient"*). This is:

1. **The first competitor-naming price-comparison ad in the farm** — every Hims/Ro/OpenAI/Anthropic ad to date either ships the three-bullet [[dtc-telehealth-ad-template]] (which never names a competitor's price) or a `{{product.brand}}` placeholder. Rx Pros breaks that pattern entirely.
2. **A near-exact template for a Medvi price-anchored compete creative** — name the incumbents' prices → state your lower price → assert active-ingredient equivalence → punch the contrast (*"Different price tag"*). → see [[glp1-price-undercut]].
3. **A methodology lesson**: the bare-brand-substring filter labels this "noise" (matched on "Pros," not a tracked page), yet by creative-intel value it outranks every placeholder "signal" ad in the farm. **The signal/noise dichotomy is orthogonal to creative-intel value** — a compounded-GLP-1 challenger with no tracked-brand name can be the single most strategically relevant advertiser the farm surfaces. The fix isn't a tighter filter (page-ID allow-listing would have *dropped* this ad); it's a **separate compounded-GLP-1-challenger watch-list** (Rx Pros, Mochi, + whatever else surfaces) alongside the tracked-brand allow-list.

**Mochi** ([[glp1-price-undercut]]) enters the vault here as a named compounded-GLP-1 competitor for the first time — surfaced not by the farm's tracking but by a rival's price-comparison copy.
