### #1 Nate B Jones — *Microsoft Says 86% Treat AI Output as a Starting Point. Your Resume Just Stopped Working* (26th framework)

**23.1K views, 2026-05-31, 10:34. → New concept: [[portable-judgment]]. Updates: [[nate-b-jones]] (26th framework), [[prove-it-economy]] (worker/career-evidence extension), [[public-ai-work]].**

His **26th named framework** — the **career-evidence** framework for the AI age. Signature reframe: *"the common story is that AI makes us more productive — but the reality is that AI makes **everyone look productive**, and the old career evidence is **losing signal**."*

**The unlock stat**: **Microsoft says 86% of workers treat AI output as a starting point**, not a finished product. If almost everyone is editing AI drafts, then a polished artifact no longer proves the person *understood* the work — output stopped carrying the signal it used to.

**Chapter map**:
- 0:00 The stat that changes career evidence
- 1:20 **Why the AI age is the age of whiteboards**
- 2:45 The old evidence stopped carrying signal
- 3:50 Why standard portfolio advice falls short
- 5:10 **Four questions that make judgment portable**
- 6:38 How whiteboard sessions reveal how you learn
- 7:30 **Comprehension over generation**
- 8:10 Starting strong in a new role
- 9:25 Making reasoning visible without a physical board
- 9:55 The evidence people actually need now

**Core claims**:
- **Polished artifacts no longer prove you understood the work** (3:50) — standard "build a portfolio" advice falls short because the portfolio is now cheap to fake with AI.
- **The AI age is the age of whiteboards** (1:20) — whiteboard conversations reveal *real judgment under pressure*; they show how you reason in real time, which AI output cannot fake.
- **Four questions that make your thinking portable and shareable** (5:10) — a reusable diagnostic for capturing reasoning so it travels beyond a single role (specific four questions gated to the Substack post / transcript).
- **Comprehension over generation** (7:30) — the durable signal is whether you *comprehend* the work, not whether you can *generate* an artifact; generation is commoditized.
- **Preserve the evidence before your badge stops working** (9:25–9:55) — capture reasoning artifacts (the "whiteboard" made durable) *before* you leave a role and lose access; making reasoning visible without a physical board is the practical move.

**Substack monetization**: *"Full Post w/ Prompts and TalentBoard link"* (`natesnewsletter.substack.com/...`) — links his **TalentBoard** product (first surfaced alongside T/C/L/D as the productized version of his worker-side frameworks).

**Why it matters**: extends his cadence to **26 frameworks in 28 days** (T/C/L/D 2026-05-04 → [[portable-judgment]] 2026-05-31). This is the **worker/career-evidence extension** of [[prove-it-economy]]'s "prove-it economy for individuals" chapter — where prove-it-economy named *verifiable artifacts + public reasoning* for individuals at the brand layer, this names the **judgment-evidence problem** at the career/hiring layer. Pairs with T/C/L/D (which tags *what* work is durable) by answering *how you prove* you do the durable work. → New concept: [[portable-judgment]].

### #2 Nate B Jones — *My AI Workflow Has Changed (Here is What I Learned)*

**49.2K views, 2026-05-30, 5:39 — highest-view video in the batch. → Updates: [[nate-b-jones]], [[ai-question-method]], [[project-room-workflow]], [[codex]], [[free-sample-phase]], [[claude-code-vs-codex]].**

A **practice-update** video (not a numbered framework) — Nate's own mid-2026 working pattern after pushing [[codex]] and [[claude-code]] to their limits. Framing: *"the common story is that prompt engineering is still the skill that separates beginners from experts — but the reality in mid-2026 is more nuanced, and the way serious AI users work has shifted dramatically in just the last few weeks."*

**Chapter map**:
- 0:00 How Nate is using AI this week
- 0:15 **Context windows and local files**
- 1:18 Long documents, spreadsheets, and code
- 2:23 **Prompting has shifted**
- 3:13 **Shape the task before execution**
- 4:26 Multi-threaded drafting and review
- 5:13 **Why not to pick a side**

**Core moves**:
- **Build local file folders as context windows** (0:15) — the filesystem is the context window; you assemble context as files, not as one long prompt. Same architecture as [[project-room-workflow]] ("files as the canvas") and the [[karpathy-llm-wiki]] `raw/`→`wiki/` pattern.
- **Codex handles 50,000-word documents other tools can't** (1:18) — concrete long-context datapoint for [[codex]]; long documents + spreadsheets + code are now in scope.
- **Prompting has shifted** (2:23) → **shape the task before execution** (3:13) — the move from *giving instructions* to *shaping the work alongside the model*. This is [[ai-question-method]]'s Principle 2 (*ask what good looks like*) restated as a practice habit: define the task shape before the agent runs.
- **Multi-threaded drafting and review** (4:26) — running multiple drafting/review threads in parallel (the practice-level cousin of [[claude-code-levels]] Level 5 "parallel sessions" and [[dynamic-workflows]]' fan-out).
- **Why not to pick a side** (5:13) — explicitly *don't* commit to one substrate; use both [[claude-code]] and [[codex]]. Direct confirmation of the [[free-sample-phase]] "use both at once" defensive play and the [[claude-code-vs-codex]] vendor-agnostic stance.

**Why it matters**: at 49.2K views it outperformed his framework video (#1, 23.1K) in this batch — a process/practice video beating a framework video, which is *unusual* for him (his pattern is framework content outperforming process content). The shift-from-instructions-to-shaping thesis ties his own working habits to his prior [[ai-question-method]] + [[project-room-workflow]] frameworks; the "why not to pick a side" close confirms the substrate-agnostic position the vault tracks. Not counted as a new named framework — a practice synthesis of existing frameworks.

### #3 Nate Herk — *Claude Code Dynamic Workflows Clearly Explained*

**57.6K views, 2026-05-30, 16:31. → New concept: [[dynamic-workflows]]. Updates: [[nate-herk]], [[claude-code]], [[opus-4-8]], [[claude-skills]], [[claude-code-levels]].**

The **first dedicated coverage of Claude Code dynamic workflows** in the vault. *"[[opus-4-8]] added dynamic workflows to Claude Code, and they look a lot like skills, subagents, agent teams, and the /goal feature, so it's easy to get confused about when you'd actually reach for one."*

**Chapter map**:

- 1:40 **Skills vs Subagents vs Teams vs Workflows**
- 5:47 **Climbing the Complexity Ladder**

- 11:18 **When You Actually Need It**
- 12:17 Running a Workflow Live
- 14:08 **Ultracode Mode**
- 15:25 One Slide + Wrap-Up

**What it adds**:
- **Dynamic workflows are a new [[opus-4-8]]-era [[claude-code]] primitive** — a deterministic orchestration layer that scripts how multiple subagents run (fan-out, pipeline, verify), sitting at the *top* of the orchestration complexity ladder.
- **The complexity ladder** (5:47): **skills → subagents → agent teams → dynamic workflows** — each rung adds orchestration power and token cost. The video's central disambiguation: *which rung does this job actually need?*
- **/goal vs workflow = depth vs width** (8:39) — `/goal` goes **deep** (one long-running agent pursuing a goal); a workflow goes **wide** (many agents fanned out and synthesized). The clearest framing yet of when to reach for which.
- **The token-cost warning** (10:13) — *"one prompt burned through half my $200 monthly plan"*; workflows can spawn dozens of agents, so they're the most expensive primitive. Pairs with [[prompt-caching]] / [[agent-metering]] / [[free-sample-phase]] as substrate-economics discipline.
- **The simple gate question** (11:18) — the one question he asks before deciding if a job is even worth a workflow (don't reach for the top of the ladder by default).
- **Ultracode mode** (14:08) + **/deep-research** (15:25 wrap) — two "hidden" higher-cost modes flagged as session-limit risks if used carelessly.

**Why it matters**: confirms [[nate-herk]]'s **mainstream-news-interpreter** role for Claude releases (same shape as his Karpathy-hire 105K, Opus-4.8 101K, session-limits 87.7K coverage).
